The Neo-Puritans

(Faction Overview for “The Veil Between Twilight and Dawn”)

Identity

The Neo-Puritans are the most “classically human” of the three races—retaining the hallmarks of Cold War–era Western-NATO ideology, reinterpreted through a thousand years of cultural drift. They embody a paradox: a civilization of discipline and pragmatism, fueled by relentless economic expansion, but plagued by inefficiency and ideological rigidity.

Where the Neo-Ionians embrace adaptive rationalism and the Neo-Pythagoreans live in cosmic mysticism, the Neo-Puritans cling to order, loyalty, and institutional strength—while struggling to keep their house united.

Core Traits

Expansion Speed: Medium

Strengths:

Exceptional economic speed: can mine, spend, and build at a pace unmatched by the other factions.

Industrial powerhouses: rapid construction of factories, fleets, and infrastructure.

Resilience tools: ability to recover “lost” or isolated systems via force, propaganda, or diplomacy.

Weaknesses:

Economic inefficiency: resources mined are only worth ⅓ as much as the Neo-Ionians’.

Fragile unity: star systems cut off from the central government tend to “break away” into independent NPCs.

Highly vulnerable to propaganda campaigns from rivals, as their ideological rigidity makes them susceptible to being undermined.

Gameplay Mechanic: Breakaway Star Systems

If a Neo-Puritan system loses connection to the capital (or sufficient influence channels), it secedes and becomes an NPC “fortress state.”

Behavior:

NPC-controlled.

Does not expand outward.

Fortifies defenses and hunkers down.

Can only be reintegrated through military conquest, propaganda infiltration, or diplomatic negotiation.

This mechanic makes Neo-Puritans strategically fragile—their empire may fracture if overextended—but also uniquely rewarding for players who can skillfully project propaganda and diplomacy, not just military might.

Culture & Flavor

Government: Bureaucratic federations, with strong central authority tied to “homeworld values.”

Military: Highly organized, NATO-style fleets and disciplined armies, though inflexible in doctrine.

Economy: Relies on relentless production volume over efficiency. “Quantity is a quality all its own.”

Religion & Ideology: Neo-Puritans have quasi-religious devotion to “Order, Faith, and Freedom,” seeing themselves as guardians of human continuity.

Society: Structured, institutionalized, but restless—citizens value stability but constantly test the system’s boundaries.

Player Fantasy

The Neo-Puritans appeal to players who want to:

Build industrial juggernauts rapidly.

Maintain tight control over an empire through politics, culture, and diplomacy.

Play a balancing act between expansion and internal stability.

Experience the constant tension of holding the center together against rival propaganda and rebellious colonies.

They are the “middle road” faction—neither as efficient and cool-headed as the Neo-Ionians, nor as mystical and transcendent as the Neo-Pythagoreans. Instead, they embody humanity’s perpetual struggle: powerful, fast, but vulnerable to division.
